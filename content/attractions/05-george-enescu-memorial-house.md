## Description (RO)

Vila Luminiș din Sinaia, în cartierul Cumpătu, a fost construită în 1923–1926 pentru compozitorul George Enescu, după proiectul arhitectului Radu Dudescu. Enescu a folosit-o până în 1946 și a compus aici, iar mai târziu a donat-o statului român. Din 1995 funcționează ca Casă Memorială, iar din 2007 este o secție a Muzeului Național „George Enescu”. La intrare se află un bust din marmură al compozitorului, realizat de Ion Irimescu. Adresa este Strada Yehudi Menuhin nr. 2, Sinaia.

## Description (EN)

Luminiș Villa in Sinaia's Cumpătu district was built in 1923–1926 for the composer George Enescu, to a design by the architect Radu Dudescu. Enescu used it until 1946 and composed there, and he later donated it to the Romanian state. It has been a memorial house since 1995 and, since 2007, a branch of the "George Enescu" National Museum. A marble bust of the composer by Ion Irimescu stands at the entrance. The address is Strada Yehudi Menuhin 2, Sinaia.

The two values that are far away fall on a road and on another street, not on the villa.
- OpenStreetMap and the preferred Wikidata statement may share an origin (the statement has no reference), so their agreement shows consistency more than independence. Nobody has checked this on site yet.

## Unverified (not used)

- Year of the donation to the state: the museum's page says 1947, English and Romanian Wikipedia place it after Enescu left for Paris in 1946. The sources disagree.
- Opening hours: the museum's page says 09:00-16:00, Wednesday to Sunday; the CIMEC museum guide and OpenStreetMap say Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00-17:00. Not stored.
- The composer's Ibach piano as an exhibit: English Wikipedia says it is kept in the saloon, the museum page only says he played it there.
- The villa built to Enescu's own plan, and the attic rooms with documents: the museum's page only.
- Architectural style (Romanian Revival, neo-Romanian) and the 1995 heritage designation: English and Romanian Wikipedia only.
